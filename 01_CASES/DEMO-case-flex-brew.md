# DEMO Case: Flex Brew Packaging Simplification

Status: DEMO / Fictional
Date: DEMO
Source: DEMO fictional source. This is not a real brand case and not Harper's personal view.
Tags: DEMO, brand, packaging, choice

## What Happened

A fictional beverage brand called Flex Brew reduced its packaging system from twelve visual variants to four clearly coded product families.

## Key Decision

The key decision was not to add more visual assets. It was to reduce choices and make the remaining cues more consistent: color family, product name hierarchy, and usage occasion.

## Why It Worked

In this fictional example, the simpler system made the shelf easier to scan and helped customers understand the product families faster.

## Evidence

DEMO evidence only:

- The fictional shelf test showed faster product family recognition.
- The fictional sales team reported fewer customer questions about product differences.
- The fictional redesign kept enough distinction between product families while removing unnecessary one-off graphics.

## My Judgment

DEMO judgment only:

The case suggests that recognition can improve when a brand reduces visual noise and focuses on fewer, stronger cues.

## What Changed My Mind

DEMO only:

It challenges the assumption that more assets automatically create a richer brand system.

## Transferable Insight

A brand system may become stronger when it reduces weak variation and increases the visibility of decisive cues.
