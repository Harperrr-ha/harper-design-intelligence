# DEMO Method: Brand Choice Simplification Method

Status: DEMO / Draft Method
Date Created: DEMO
Last Updated: DEMO
Tags: DEMO, brand, method, visual diagnosis

## Purpose

DEMO method only:

Help evaluate whether a brand system has too many weak variations and identify which cues should become stronger.

## When To Use

- A product line feels visually noisy.
- Customers cannot quickly understand differences.
- The brand has many assets but weak recognition.
- A redesign brief asks for both consistency and distinction.

## Inputs

- Current packaging or visual system
- Product family structure
- Customer decision moments
- Shelf, ecommerce, or usage context
- Existing brand assets

## Steps

1. List all visible cues.

   Examples: color, typography, product name, shape, photography, icon, claim, material, layout.

2. Mark which cues help recognition.

   Separate decisive cues from decorative or one-off variation.

3. Identify confusion points.

   Ask where customers may misunderstand product family, benefit, price tier, or use occasion.

4. Reduce weak variation.

   Remove cues that create difference without meaning.

5. Strengthen decisive cues.

   Make the remaining cues easier to scan and remember.

6. Test against context.

   Check shelf, ecommerce thumbnail, social image, and real use scenario.

## Decision Criteria

- Can people identify product family faster?
- Are differences meaningful rather than decorative?
- Does the system still feel like the same brand?
- Is there enough expressive room for future campaigns?

## Common Mistakes

- Simplifying everything until the brand becomes flat.
- Treating consistency as visual sameness.
- Removing emotional cues that actually matter.
- Adding new assets before understanding existing cues.
