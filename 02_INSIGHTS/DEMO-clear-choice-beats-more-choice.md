# DEMO Insight: Clear Choice Can Beat More Choice

Status: Seed -> Insight -> Validated (DEMO chain only)
Date Created: DEMO
Last Updated: DEMO
Tags: DEMO, brand, recognition, choice

## Current Thought

DEMO thought only:

When a brand system gives people fewer but clearer visual choices, recognition may improve more than it would through adding more assets.

## Origin

This thought comes from the fictional DEMO Case: `01_CASES/DEMO-case-flex-brew.md`.

## Evidence

DEMO evidence only:

- Flex Brew simplified twelve variants into four product families.
- Fictional shelf recognition improved.
- Fictional customer confusion decreased.

## Counter Evidence

DEMO counter evidence:

- In some categories, variety itself may be part of the brand promise.
- A system can become too simplified and lose emotional richness.
- Premium or lifestyle brands may need expressive variation for storytelling.

## My Current Conclusion

DEMO conclusion only:

The useful question is not "How many assets does the brand have?" but "Which cues actually help people recognize and decide?"

## Open Questions

- When does simplification become too plain?
- Which categories need variety as part of the experience?
- How can a system reduce noise without losing texture?
